## The itch

After God says you are mine, the first thing that happens is not a blessing you can feel. It is a wilderness you did not choose.

## Why this is the hook

Found in prep, not invented.

- Your Mark 1 Notes already have it: identity, endowment, and compulsion arrive together. Then *ekballei*, present: the Spirit throws him out. Same verb Mark uses when Jesus throws demons, and when he throws the leper out (1:43). Matt *anēchthē*, Luke *ēgeto*: led. Mark will not let it be a gentle leading.
- Drive source notes (Caneday): irony — the Spirit casts out Jesus; Jesus then casts out others.
- NIV softens 1:12 to “sent him out.” Preach the Greek.

**What loses**

- Torn sky / torn veil — already spent August 30.
- Wild beasts — live split (peace vs hostility). Leave it open. Not an opening punch.
- Forty days / Israel — body, not hook.
- John’s wilderness, then Jesus’ — good turn after the verb lands. Not the first sentence.

## The Hook

I want you to hear the next two verses before your Bible has time to soften them.

Last Sunday the wilderness was where the voice showed up. A man in camel hair, standing in the Jordan, yelling that the King was coming.

This Sunday the King shows up.

He walks down from Nazareth. He takes the water. He does not explain Himself. John does not argue with Him. Heaven tears. The Spirit comes *into* Him — Mark’s word, not “on” Him — and a voice speaks *to Him*, not to the crowd:

***“You are my Son, whom I love; with you I am well pleased.”*** **(Mark 1:11)**

That is a coronation.

And if this were any other story, the next scene would be a parade.

A throne.

A speech.

Something that looks like winning.

### Mark will not give you that.

Your pew Bible may say the Spirit *sent* Him out. Matthew says *led*. Luke says *led*.

Mark’s word is **ekballei**.

That is the verb he uses when Jesus throws a demon out of a man. Same verb when He throws the leper out of the camp. Present tense. Happening in front of you.

**The Spirit throws Him.**

The same Spirit who just came into Him.

Immediately.

Into the wilderness.

Not a retreat. Not a quiet time. Not a walk in the woods to get His thoughts together.

The Father names Him Son. The Spirit fills Him. And the first thing the Spirit does with a full Son is drive Him onto enemy ground.

Alone.

Before He preaches a word.

Before He calls a disciple.

Before anybody in Capernaum has heard His name.

### Forty days.

Satan testing Him.

Wild beasts — Mark will not tell you yet whether they are company or threat.

Angels serving Him the whole time, not after He wins. Mark never writes the win in this scene. The rest of the book is the win.

Last week I told you the wilderness might be the road He’s coming down.

This week Mark tells you something harder.

**Sometimes the wilderness is not the place you wandered into.**

**Sometimes it is the place the Spirit threw you after He called you His.**
