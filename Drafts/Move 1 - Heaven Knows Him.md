# Move 1: Heaven Knows Him

**Sunday, September 6, 2026 — Straight Creek**
**Text:** Mark 1:9–11 (opening the 1:9–13 unit)

---

Let's put ourselves at the scene.

Jesus has just come up out of the water.

We know who John the Baptist is. We know who Jesus is. But the people standing at that river would most likely have recognized John more readily than Jesus.

John already had a following. He was the reason people were coming out there.

Mark 1:5 tells us

“The whole Judean countryside and all the people of Jerusalem went out to John. They came to hear him. They confessed their sins. He baptized them in the Jordan.”

That's the messenger who was prophesied to come. Mark reminds them of what God has said about the messenger in the wilderness who would prepare the way. And he makes it clear that it’s this man standing in the water. Then he describes him:

The camel's hair.
The leather belt.
The locusts and wild honey.
Everything he described would have brought Elijah to mind.

Then we get to verse 9.

**9** In those days Jesus came from Nazareth in Galilee and was baptized by John in the Jordan.

Mark doesn't stop to give us His family history. He gives us His name and His hometown, and then John baptizes Him in the Jordan.

John, doing messenger type things, has already said someone stronger is coming. Someone whose sandal strap he isn't worthy to stoop down and untie. John baptizes with water. The One coming after him will baptize with the Holy Spirit.

Then the stronger One walks into the scene.

And Mark never has John point and say, "This is Him."

We get John's testimony identifying Jesus in the Gospel of John. But watch how Mark tells this scene.

Jesus comes up out of the water. He sees the heavens being torn open. The Spirit descends on Him like a dove.

And the Father speaks.

**11** and a voice came from the heavens: “You are My beloved Son; in You I am well pleased.”

## So Personal

“You are My Son. I love You. I delight in You.”

Mark confirms His identity through the Father's voice, with the Spirit descending upon Him.

We haven't watched Jesus train as a rabbi. We haven't seen Him as a twelve-year-old in the temple, like Luke shows us. Mark hasn't even given us a sermon from Jesus.

What we get first is the Father's declaration of who He is.

Imagine you're standing there at the river. You probably came to hear John preach. Perhaps you've already confessed your sins and been baptized. Then you watch this man from Galilee go into the water.

He comes up.

What do you see? What do you hear?

Mark doesn't tell us what the crowd saw or heard. He tells us that Jesus saw the heavens being torn open.

What we see is:

A descending Spirit
The Father's voice speaking to His Son.

The crowds may have come for John.

But heaven opens up for Jesus.
