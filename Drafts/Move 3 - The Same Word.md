# Move 3: The Same Word

**Sunday, September 6, 2026 — Straight Creek**
**Text:** Mark 1:12, read against Genesis 3:23–24
**Job:** Disclose the clue that answers why the same Spirit who descended on the Beloved drives Him into the wilderness to be tempted.

---

Now I want to sit with the question Move 2 left on the table.

Why?

Why would the same Spirit who just came down on Jesus as Beloved send Him into the wilderness to be tempted?

We already know it is not punishment.
We already know the Father has not taken His love back.
We already know heaven has not closed.

So why this?

Mark does not stop to explain it.
He just puts the two sentences next to each other and keeps walking.

Beloved.
Then driven.

I want to show you a clue.
It will not answer every question you have about suffering.
It will tell you what this Spirit is doing with this Son.

I need to take you back before Mark.
All the way back to the first time somebody got driven out.

## East of Eden

Genesis 3.

Adam and Eve have sinned.
The ground is cursed.
Death has entered the story.

And God does something that changes the geography of the human race.

Genesis 3:23–24 says,

“So the Lord God banished him from the Garden of Eden to work the ground from which he had been taken. After he drove the man out, he placed on the east side of the Garden of Eden cherubim and a flaming sword flashing back and forth to guard the way to the tree of life.”

He drove the man out.

Then He put a guard at the door.
Cherubim.
A sword that will not let them back in.

From that moment on, the human story is a story lived east of Eden.

Outside the garden.
Outside the place where heaven and earth met.
In a world of thorns and sweat and graves.

Then their son Cain kills his brother.
And Genesis 4:16 says,

“So Cain went out from the Lord’s presence and lived in the land of Nod, east of Eden.”

The human race keeps moving farther from the garden.

Eden is behind us.
We live outside the garden.

## The Word Mark Uses

Now come back to Mark.

Jesus has just come up out of the water.
The heavens have been torn open.
The Spirit has descended on Him like a dove.
The Father has said, “You are My beloved Son; in You I am well pleased.”

And then Mark writes,

“And immediately the Spirit brought Him out into the wilderness.”

Here is what caught me.

If you read Genesis in the Greek Bible Mark knew, Genesis says God drove Adam out.

Mark uses the same word.

God drove Adam out.
The Spirit drives Jesus out.

I do not think Mark expects us to build a whole Adam sermon on one word.
I am not standing here to tell you he is rewriting Genesis 3 line by line.

But I cannot miss the echo.

And the echo is not that they are doing the same thing.

Look at the difference.

Adam is driven out after he fails the test.
Jesus is driven out to face the test.

The first man is driven from the garden because he listened to the serpent.
The beloved Son is driven into the wilderness to meet the serpent.

Adam is sent away from the presence.
Jesus is sent from the Father’s delight straight into the place Adam was sent.

That is the clue.

## Why the Beloved Goes

So why does the same Spirit who named Him Beloved lead Him into temptation?

Because love did not come to call us back to Eden from a safe distance.

The Spirit is not contradicting the Father.
The Spirit is carrying out the Father’s love.

The Father has just claimed this Son.
And the first thing that love does is send Him into our country.

Not away from the Father.
Into the place where we are.

For generations, humanity had lived outside the garden.
Then the Son of God stepped into our wilderness.

That is why Beloved and driven can sit in the same breath.

He is not driven because the Father is done delighting in Him.
He is driven because the Father delights in Him, and this is what the beloved Son came to do.

He came east of Eden to get us.

The wilderness is not the cancellation of the baptism.
It is the baptism going somewhere.

The Spirit comes upon Him.
And the Spirit’s first assignment is the country we already live in.

Satan will meet Him there.
The wild animals are there.
The angels are there.

Mark is not done telling you what all of that means.
We are not going to settle every piece of verse 13 this morning.

But you already have the clue that holds the question.

The same Spirit who descended on the Beloved drives Him into the wilderness because that wilderness is not a detour from His identity.

It is the first step of it.

The Father loves Him at the water.
The Spirit sends Him into our exile.
And the Son goes as the Beloved.

That is who is standing in the wilderness.

Not a man God has rejected.
The Son God has sent.
