# Move 2: Where the Spirit Sends Him

**Sunday, September 6, 2026 — Straight Creek**
**Text:** Mark 1:12–13 (still inside the 1:9–13 unit)

---

Mark will not let us stay at the river.

The Father has just called Him beloved. The Spirit has just come upon Him. And the next word is not a pause.

**12** At once the Spirit sent him out into the wilderness,

**13** and he was in the wilderness forty days, being tempted by Satan. He was with the wild animals, and angels attended him.

Same Spirit.

The Spirit who came down is the Spirit who sends Him out.

Immediately.

Matthew and Luke will say the Spirit led Him. Mark will not. Mark's word is stronger than a gentle leading. The Spirit drives Him.

This is not a detour from His mission. This is where His mission takes Him.

Israel came through that river to leave the wilderness behind.

Jesus comes through that river, and the Spirit sends Him into it.

The Father has just delighted in Him.

Then the same Spirit sends Him toward Satan.

Beloved in one verse. Driven in the next.

Same love.
Same Spirit.

Forty days.

Mark does not give us three conversations in the sand. He does not walk us through the tests the way Matthew and Luke will. He gives us the conflict itself.

He was in the wilderness forty days, being tempted by Satan.

That word can mean tempted. It can mean tested. Mark will not let us sand it down. Satan is not a mood. Satan is the adversary. The stronger One has entered the wilderness, and the fight is already on.

He was with the wild animals.

Mark puts Him there and does not explain the line. He does not tell us they attacked. He does not tell us they lay down. He just puts the beloved Son in the wild.

And angels attended Him.

Not after He wins. Mark does not write a victory scene here and then send help. The testing and the attending stand in the same breath. Satan opposes Him. Heaven serves Him. He is not abandoned.

Mark does not tell us He won this round.

He tells us He was there.

Forty days.
Tempted by Satan.
With the wild animals.
Angels attending Him.

The Father's pleasure and the wilderness stand right beside each other.

The Son is loved when He enters the water.
He is still loved when He enters the wilderness.

Face where the Spirit sends Him.

Not away from the Father.
Into the conflict His mission takes Him into.

The Spirit who comes upon Jesus at the water is the Spirit who drives Jesus into the wilderness.
