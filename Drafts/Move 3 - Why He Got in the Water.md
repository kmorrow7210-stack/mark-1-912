# Move 3: Why He Got in the Water

**Sunday, September 6, 2026 — Straight Creek**
**Text:** Mark 1:9, closing the 1:9–13 unit

---

## The Question

Before we leave this chapter, I need to walk you back to the water.

Because I've been holding a question all morning. And if it hasn't crossed your mind yet, it will on the way home.

Go back to verse 4 with me.

**4** "And so John the Baptist appeared in the wilderness, preaching a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins."

Mark tells us what this baptism is for before anybody gets in the water. Repentance. Forgiveness of sins.

Verse 5. The whole Judean countryside comes out. And look at what they do.

**5** "Confessing their sins, they were baptized by him in the Jordan River."

That's the order. Confess, then go under.

Now come back to verse 9.

**9** "At that time Jesus came from Nazareth in Galilee and was baptized by John in the Jordan."

Same river. Same man doing the baptizing. Same baptism.

But Mark never says Jesus confessed anything.

And he never will. Read the whole book. Later in this very chapter an unclean spirit takes one look at Him and hollers, "I know who you are—the Holy One of God!" In chapter 14 they go hunting for something to charge Him with, and Mark says it plain: they did not find any. In chapter 15, Pilate stands in front of a mob and asks, "Why? What crime has he committed?"

Nobody in this Gospel can find a sin on Jesus.

And the church never found one either. From the beginning, this has been one of those things we do not negotiate. He is without sin.

So here's my question. And I want you to actually feel it before I try to answer it.

**If He has nothing to confess, what is He doing in a river for the forgiveness of sins?**

## Chuck Norris Doesn't Get Wet

We lost Chuck Norris this past March. Eighty-six years old.

And say what you want about the movies. Before he was ever on television, that man was a real martial artist and a real champion. He was a legend.

But you know what outlived the movies? The jokes.

Chuck Norris had a staring contest with the sun. And won.

And my favorite one:

**When Chuck Norris goes swimming, he doesn't get wet. The water gets Chuck Norris.**

Now I didn't tell you that just to hear you laugh. I told you that because that is exactly how I have heard us answer this question about Jesus.

"Well, Jesus didn't need that water. That water needed Jesus. He didn't get washed — He washed the river."

And listen, part of that is true and I don't want to lose it. He is not standing in the Jordan because He had a list. He is the Holy One of God. John said in verse 7 that the One coming behind him is stronger than he is.

But if that's where I stop, do you know what I've done?

I've turned my Savior into a Chuck Norris joke.

Untouchable. Unbothered. Immune. Standing in the Jordan and staying dry.

Church, an immune Savior can't save anybody.

So look at what Mark actually wrote. Verse 9 does not say the water got Jesus. It says Jesus *was baptized*. He went down into the Jordan. In the same line. In the same water everybody else had just confessed into.

**He got wet.**

And He's not done getting wet either. Verse 12, the Spirit drives Him out into the desert. Chapter 14, they arrest Him. Chapter 15, they beat Him and mock Him and nail Him to a cross, and He dies.

There is nothing immune about any of that.

That's not weakness.

That's willingness.

## The Stronger One Goes in the House

Hold on to that word John used in verse 7. *Stronger.*

Your Bible may say "more powerful." Same root. And Mark picks that word right back up in chapter 3.

They've accused Jesus of doing His work by the devil's power. And He answers them with a picture.

**"In fact, no one can enter a strong man's house without first tying him up. Then he can plunder the strong man's house."** (Mark 3:27)

You want to take a strong man's house?

You can't do it from the yard.

You have to go in.

Now read verse 12 again. The Spirit drives Him out into the wilderness, and He spends forty days with Satan.

That's Him walking in the house.

The stronger One does not prove He's strong by staying out of the water and staying out of the desert.

He proves it by going in.

## Whose Country Is That?

And think about where the Spirit sends Him.

Turn all the way back to Genesis 3 with me. Adam and Eve have eaten. The ground is cursed. Death is in the story now. And Genesis says God *drove the man out*, and put cherubim on the east side of the garden with a flaming sword flashing back and forth, to guard the way back to the tree of life.

That's where we've lived ever since. Outside. East of Eden. Thorns and sweat and funerals.

Here's the part I can't shake.

In the Greek Bible that Mark's first hearers read, the word for God driving Adam out of that garden is the same word Mark uses in verse 12 for the Spirit driving Jesus out into the wilderness.

Same verb.

Now, I'm not going to build a whole doctrine on one word. But I can't unhear it, either.

**Adam was driven out because he failed the test.**
**Jesus was driven out to face the test.**

Adam walked out of there under a verdict.

Jesus walks out there as the Son the Father just called beloved.

And He goes to the exact place our disobedience put us.

So the wilderness is not a detour on the way to His ministry.

The wilderness is our address.

He did not stand in the garden and call to us across the fence. He came out here where we live.

## Forty Days Where We Had Forty Years

And He came out here to do something we have never done.

Deuteronomy 8. Moses is looking back over the whole thing, and here is how he says it:

**"Remember how the Lord your God led you all the way in the wilderness these forty years, to humble and test you in order to know what was in your heart, whether or not you would keep his commands."**

Forty years. Wilderness. Tested.

And we know how that went. They grumbled. They built a calf. They stood at the edge of the land and refused to go in. And that generation died out there in the sand.

Now look at verse 13.

**13** "and he was in the wilderness forty days, being tempted by Satan."

Forty days. Wilderness. Tested.

Mark doesn't give us the play-by-play. He doesn't tell us the three things Satan said — you have to go over to Matthew and Luke for that. Mark tells us where Jesus was, who was testing Him, and that He was out there the whole forty.

And then in verse 14, He walks out of that desert and starts preaching.

No failure recorded. No apology. No second attempt.

**Israel went into the wilderness and fell.**
**The beloved Son went into the wilderness and stayed.**

And He kept staying. All the way to a garden called Gethsemane, where He got down on the ground and said, "Take this cup from me" — and then said, "Yet not what I will, but what you will."

Faithful in the desert. Faithful in the garden. Faithful all the way up the hill.

Everywhere we quit, He didn't.

## The Baptism He Was Really Walking Toward

Now let me show you the thing that finally answered my question.

Jesus uses this baptism word again.

Chapter 10. He is walking to Jerusalem. He has just told the Twelve for the third time that He is going to be handed over and condemned and killed, and that three days later He will rise. And right then James and John come up and ask for the good seats.

And Jesus says this to them:

**"You don't know what you are asking. Can you drink the cup I drink or be baptized with the baptism I am baptized with?"** (Mark 10:38)

Now what baptism is that?

He's already been baptized. That happened back in chapter 1.

He's talking about His death.

**Jesus calls His own death a baptism.**

So go back to the river with me. When He steps down into that Jordan, that is not a strange way to start the story.

That's the first step down.

Down into our water. Down into our wilderness. Down into our death.

**"For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many."** (Mark 10:45)

**"This is my blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many."** (Mark 14:24)

He got in the water with sinners, and He ended up on a cross between two criminals.

And remember what we saw at that river. Verse 10. The heavens were *torn* open.

That word shows up one other time in the whole Gospel of Mark. Chapter 15. Jesus breathes His last, and "the curtain of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom."

Heaven tore open over Him at the water.

The veil tore open the day He died.

Same rip. Same curtain we stood in front of last Sunday.

At the river, the Father says, "You are my Son, whom I love." At the cross, a Roman soldier who watched Him die says it out loud: "Surely this man was the Son of God!"

And then Sunday morning comes. Three women with spices, worried about who's going to move a stone. And a young man sitting in the tomb says to them:

**"He has risen! He is not here. See the place where they laid him."**

Last Sunday we stood with Pompey in an empty room.

This morning I want you to stand with those women at an empty tomb.

The empty room said heaven had nothing left to say to us.

The empty tomb says the way is open.

## So What Happened in Your Water?

Which brings me to the water you got in.

A lot of you have been baptized. And if I asked what happened that day, most of us would say, "I got my sins washed away."

That's true, and thank God for it. But look at what Mark puts in front of you.

Verse 8. John says, **"I baptize you with water, but he will baptize you with the Holy Spirit."**

That Spirit. The one that came down on Jesus at the river. That's the promise attached to you.

And then chapter 10 again. Jesus asks James and John if they can be baptized with His baptism. They say, "We can." And watch what He tells them:

**"You will drink the cup I drink and be baptized with the baptism I am baptized with."**

He shares it with them.

His cup. His baptism. His people.

So your baptism is not you handing God a clean report card.

Your baptism says you belong to Him.

You went down where He went down. You came up where He came up. And whatever you are walking into, He walked into it first and He is still out in front of you.

That's exactly what the young man told the women at the tomb: **"He is going ahead of you into Galilee. There you will see him."**

He is already up the road.

## Then Go Tell It

And that belonging has to walk out of this building.

Watch Mark. Nobody in this Gospel comes up out of the water and sits down.

Jesus comes out of the wilderness preaching: "The time has come. The kingdom of God has come near. Repent and believe the good news!"

Verse 17, He walks up on two fishermen and says, "Come, follow me, and I will send you out to fish for people."

Over in chapter 5, a man Jesus has just set free begs to go with Him. And Jesus tells him, "Go home to your own people and tell them how much the Lord has done for you." Mark says the man went out and told it all over the Decapolis.

That's the assignment, church. Go home and tell what He did for you.

And in chapter 13 Jesus is honest with them that it won't always be welcome. He says you'll stand in front of governors and kings. And then He says, "Do not worry beforehand about what to say... for it is not you speaking, but the Holy Spirit."

Which Spirit?

The one from the river.

Same Spirit. He is still the One doing the sending. And He does the talking.

## Close

So let me put the question back on the table.

If Jesus is sinless, why was He baptized at all?

Because He never had any intention of saving us from a distance.

He stepped down into the water our sin had muddied.
He walked out into the country our disobedience made.
He was tested where we had already failed, and He held.
He called His own death a baptism, and He went down into that too.
And on the third day, that tomb was empty and the road home was open.

**He didn't stay dry.**

So when you go down in that water, you are not cleaning yourself up for God. You are saying: I belong to Him. Where He went, I'm going. What He came up into, I'm coming up into.

*[Optional callback to the hook — use only if the hibachi opening stays.]*
I stood up here this morning and told you I skipped the preparation.

He didn't skip anything.

Not the water. Not the wilderness. Not the cross.

## The Altar

So this altar is open.

If you have never gone down in that water, come.

If you went down in it and you've been living like you don't belong to Him, come.

If you are standing in a wilderness this morning and you need to hear that the Father has not changed His mind about you, come.

And if you're His and you know it, then go home and tell somebody how much the Lord has done for you. That's the whole assignment.

He got in the water with us.

He came up out of the grave ahead of us.

Come on.

## Preparation Notes and Sources

[Every quotation below was checked against a text, not from memory. Mark and the Old Testament are quoted from the NIV to match the working chapter text in the project (`Mark 1.md`); Move 2 quotes 1:12 from the KJV, so decide which voice you want for 1:12 if you read it again here.

**The question itself.** This is the open item recorded in the Notion workspace under Mark 1:9–11: "If Jesus is sinless, why is he baptized?" Mark 1:4 defines John's baptism as "a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins" and 1:5 says the crowds were baptized "confessing their sins." Mark 1:9 says Jesus was baptized in the same river by the same man. Mark reports no confession by Jesus. That silence is the whole hinge, and it is a real feature of the text, not an argument from absence being oversold — Mark states the purpose of the rite in v. 4 and then omits the confession in v. 9.

**Sinlessness, kept inside Mark.** Three Markan witnesses instead of an outside proof text: the unclean spirit calls Him "the Holy One of God" (1:24, ὁ ἅγιος τοῦ θεοῦ — see `Mark 1_24 Parsed.md`); the Sanhedrin "were looking for evidence against Jesus so that they could put him to death, but they did not find any" (14:55); Pilate asks, "Why? What crime has he committed?" (15:14). Note the honest limits: 1:24 is a confession of holiness on the lips of a demon, and 14:55 and 15:14 are about legal innocence, not a formal doctrine of sinlessness. The doctrinal statement itself is Hebrews 4:15 ("yet he did not sin") and 2 Corinthians 5:21. The draft therefore says the church has never negotiated it and leaves the proof texts outside the pulpit, which keeps the promise to stay in Mark.

**Chuck Norris — one correction.** He died **March 19, 2026**, at 86, in Hawaii; the family announced it March 20 and kept the cause private. So "we lost him last year" is off by a year. The draft says "this past March," which is right and also lands better six months out. Public memorial was July 18, 2026, at the Rio in Las Vegas, folded into the United Fighting Arts Federation conference he founded in 1979. Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Norris), [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c0le8e5jg1xo), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/20/entertainment/chuck-norris-death), [AP via The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/entertainment/chuck-norris-dies-at-86-action-star-martial-arts-grandmaster/article70766568.ece). The "staring contest with the sun" joke is the one the AP obituary itself quotes, so it is safe to attribute to the meme. Your swimming line is the load-bearing one and it is the setup for the turn, so it stays as you wrote it.

**Why the joke works here.** It is a foil, not an illustration of the point. The laugh establishes the wrong answer — invulnerability — and then verse 9 takes it away. Guard the theology on the way through: the turn is not that Jesus was less than sinless or less than God, only that He was not immune. "That's not weakness. That's willingness." If a hearer could walk out thinking Jesus needed cleansing, the turn has gone too far; as written, "He is not standing in the Jordan because He had a list" blocks that.

If you want the ancient version of the Chuck Norris answer, Ignatius says Jesus was baptized "that by his passion he might purify the water" (*Ephesians* 18:2). That is outside Mark, and it also proves the point you are making — even the tradition that says He sanctified the water ties it straight to His passion. Left out of the manuscript on purpose.

**"Stronger" (1:7) and the strong man (3:27).** Mark 1:7 has ὁ ἰσχυρότερός μου; Mark 3:27 has τοῦ ἰσχυροῦ and τὸν ἰσχυρὸν δήσῃ. Same root, so this is a genuine repeated Markan term and not an English coincidence. But the NIV renders 1:7 "more powerful" and 3:27 "strong man," which hides the link in English — that is why the draft says "your Bible may say more powerful, same root." Two cautions: identifying the strong man as Satan is the standard reading of the Beelzebul controversy, but Mark does not name him; and εἰσελθὼν ("having entered") genuinely carries the "you have to go in" point, so that line rests on the text.

**Optional extra beat if you want it (Mark 5:3–4).** The Gerasene man "no one could bind (δῆσαι) him anymore" and "no one was strong enough (ἴσχυεν) to subdue him" — the same two verbs as 3:27, where the strong man must first be bound. And that is the man Jesus sends home to tell it (5:19–20). It ties the strength thread to the witness thread in one stroke. Cut for length, not for accuracy.

**Genesis 3 and ἐκβάλλω.** NIV Genesis 3:24: "After he drove the man out, he placed on the east side of the Garden of Eden cherubim and a flaming sword flashing back and forth to guard the way to the tree of life." LXX Genesis 3:24 opens καὶ ἐξέβαλεν τὸν Αδαμ (confirmed against Blue Letter Bible's LXX and against your own `Genesis 3:24 Hebrew and LXX` note in Notion; Hebrew is piel of גרש). Mark 1:12 has τὸ πνεῦμα αὐτὸν ἐκβάλλει. Same lemma, ἐκβάλλω. J. P. Heil (*CBQ* 68, 2006 — the local `Heil2006_JesusWithWildAnimals.pdf`) puts exactly this contrast on the table and uses it *against* a simple "Jesus as new Adam restoring paradise" reading, because Adam is expelled *after* failing and Jesus is driven out *before* being tested. The draft keeps Heil's distinction rather than flattening it, and says out loud that it will not build a doctrine on one word. Do not claim Mark is consciously retelling Genesis 3.

**Forty days and forty years.** Deuteronomy 8:2 quoted in full and verified. Note what the draft does *not* do: Mark never quotes Deuteronomy 8. Matthew and Luke do (Deut 8:3 at Matthew 4:4 / Luke 4:4). So this is offered as an Old Testament pattern of wilderness testing, which is what you allowed, not as a Markan citation. Israel's failures referenced are the calf (Exodus 32) and the refusal to enter with that generation dying in the wilderness (Numbers 14) — accurate, though the calf belongs to Sinai rather than to the wandering years, so keep it general as written.

Mark 1:13 says He was there forty days being tempted by Satan. Mark narrates no outcome — no reported victory, no quoted exchange. The draft is careful here: "No failure recorded" is a claim about Mark's silence, and the forward movement is supplied by 1:14, where He comes out preaching. Do not import Matthew's or Luke's three temptations.

**The Jordan reversal** (Israel wilderness → Jordan → land; Jesus Jordan → wilderness) is already worked out in your Notion Jordan typology page and sits under this move implicitly. It is not re-argued here because Move 2 carries it.

**Baptism as death — the load-bearing link.** Mark 10:38–39 uses Mark's own baptism vocabulary for the cross: τὸ βάπτισμα ὃ ἐγὼ βαπτίζομαι βαπτισθῆναι, and then βαπτισθήσεσθε of the disciples. This is what lets the move connect the Jordan to Calvary without leaving Mark, and it is also what lets "our baptism joins us to him" stay inside Mark — Jesus Himself says the disciples will share His cup and His baptism (10:39). Romans 6:3–4 states the doctrine directly and your Notion note already reaches for it; it is deliberately left out of the manuscript to honor the Mark-only rule. Add it in one sentence if you want the doctrine named.

Third passion prediction with "three days later he will rise" is Mark 10:33–34, immediately before the James and John request, which is why the draft sets the scene that way. Mark 10:45 (ransom for many) and 14:24 (blood of the covenant poured out for many) verified. Gethsemane is 14:36, quoted exactly.

**The tearing.** Forms of σχίζω occur at Mark 1:10 and 15:38 and nowhere else in Mark; that link was established in the earlier notes and is reused here as a one-line callback, plus the centurion's confession at 15:39 answering the Father's voice at 1:11. The "empty room / empty tomb" line is the only callback to August 30, kept to two sentences per the guardrail against rerunning last Sunday.

**Resurrection.** Mark 16:6 ("He has risen! He is not here. See the place where they laid him") and 16:7 ("go, tell his disciples and Peter, 'He is going ahead of you into Galilee'") — both verified, both from the undisputed ending. Nothing in this move depends on 16:9–20. "He is already up the road" renders προάγει.

**Witness.** Mark 1:14–15, 1:17, 5:19–20, 13:9–11 all quoted from verified text. κηρύσσω is a genuine Markan through-line: John preaches (1:4, 1:7), Jesus preaches (1:14, 1:38–39), the cleansed leper preaches (1:45), the gospel is preached to all nations (13:10). That repetition is what justifies "nobody in this Gospel comes up out of the water and sits down." Mark 6:7–13 (the Twelve sent out two by two, preaching repentance) is available if you want a fourth example.

**One text-critical note you probably do not need out loud.** Mark 1:9 reads ἐβαπτίσθη **εἰς** τὸν Ἰορδάνην — "into the Jordan" — while 1:5 has the crowds baptized **ἐν** τῷ Ἰορδάνῃ ποταμῷ. It is tempting to preach "they were baptized *in* it, He went *into* it," but εἰς and ἐν overlap in Koine and most commentators read 1:9 as simply "in." The draft says "He went down into the Jordan" as plain narration and does not hang anything on the preposition. Leave it there.

**One conflict with the earlier notes.** The Move 2 notes reserved Mark 1:14 ("After John was put in prison, Jesus went into Galilee, proclaiming the good news of God") so that Move 2 would not anticipate the next scene. Move 3 uses 1:14 twice — once to show that Jesus walks out of the desert still preaching, and once at the head of the witness section. That is the right home for it, since Move 3 is the close and nothing before it has spent the verse. But if Move 2 grows and takes 1:14, cut it here.

**Things this move deliberately does not do.** It does not explain the wild animals. It does not decide whether the angels arrived early or late. It does not import John the Baptist's "Lamb of God" from the Fourth Gospel, which would answer the question faster but from outside Mark. It does not quote Romans 6, Hebrews 4:15, or 2 Corinthians 5:21 in the pulpit.

**Delivery.** The laugh and the turn are one unit — do not let applause or laughter settle before "I told you that because that is exactly how I have heard us answer this question." The two slowest lines in the move are "He got wet" and "He didn't stay dry." Both need silence after them. The Genesis 3 section is the one place a hearer can get lost; land it on "the wilderness is our address" and move.]
