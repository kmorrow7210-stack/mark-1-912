### Syntax

**πολλοὺς κακῶς ἔχοντας** literally means:  
“many who were doing badly”  
But **κακῶς ἔχω** is an idiom meaning “to be sick”:  
“many who were sick”  
**ποικίλαις νόσοις** is a dative of respect or cause:  
“sick with various diseases.”  
**οὐκ ἤφιεν λαλεῖν τὰ δαιμόνια** uses an object-plus-infinitive construction:

* **τὰ δαιμόνια** — the ones not permitted  
* **λαλεῖν** — what they were not permitted to do

The imperfect **ἤφιεν** presents this as Jesus’ continuing policy:  
“He was not allowing the demons to speak.”  
**ὅτι ᾔδεισαν αὐτόν** gives the reason:  
“because they knew him.”  
Here **ᾔδεισαν** is formally pluperfect, but **οἶδα** has a present sense, “I know.” Its pluperfect therefore carries the ordinary past sense, “they knew.”  
A smooth translation:  
“And he healed many who were sick with various diseases, cast out many demons, and would not allow the demons to speak, because they knew him.”

### Syntax

The opening expressions accumulate to emphasize the hour:  
**πρωῒ ἔννυχα λίαν**“very early, while it was still night.”  
**ἀναστάς** is a temporal participle:  
“after getting up” or “having risen.”  
The main verbal sequence is:  
**ἐξῆλθεν καὶ ἀπῆλθεν**“he went out and went away.”  
**ἔρημον τόπον** does not necessarily mean a sandy desert. It means an uninhabited, solitary, or deserted place.  
Finally, **προσηύχετο** is imperfect. After the aorist verbs carry Jesus to the place, the imperfect depicts the continuing activity there:  
“and there he was praying.”  
A smooth translation:  
“Very early, while it was still night, he got up, went out, and departed to a solitary place; and there he was praying.”
