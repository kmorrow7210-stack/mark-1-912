## **Malachi 4:4-6**

***4 Remember ye the law of Moses my servant, which I commanded unto him in Horeb for all Israel, with the statutes and judgments.***

***5 Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the Lord:***

***6 And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse.***

## **The Hook**

I see the irony in beginning a series on Mark by opening with a reading of the last line of the last chapter of the last book in the Old Testament.

I think it's important to see how it ended. In Malachi 4, verse 6, Malachi is standing there and God gives him the closing word:

***“lest I come and strike the land with a curse."***

That word in the Hebrew is \*\*\**herem*\*\*\*. It doesn't mean bad luck. It doesn't mean a rough season.

#### **\- \*Herem\* is what happened to Jericho.**

* #### Devoted to destruction. 

* #### Handed over. 

* #### Blotted out.

*That is the word God chooses to go out on.*

**And then he just …. stops talking.**

Not for a week. Not for a season.

### **For four hundred years.**

Think about what that would have been like.   
A man hears that last line as a boy. He grows up, gets married, buries his father, raises his sons, tells them what the prophet said, and dies waiting.

His sons wait. Their sons wait. Fourteen generations of people going up to the temple, watching the smoke go up, listening, and nothing comes back down.

No prophet.

No dream.

No voice.

Just the same last sentence that sounds an awful lot like a threat.

#### **And they're not sitting on their hands.**

### **They're expecting.** 

They're guessing. Counting the signs. Every so often somebody with a sword, a following and some charisma rises up and the whole country leans in. 

A man who says he's the one. And for a minute it looks like he may be right.

Then he takes the throne and turns into the tyrant he promised to fight against. Or Rome finds him. And the crowd scatters, and the country goes back to waiting.

Nobody in that four hundred years knew which one was coming next.

The prophet, or the curse. Because heaven had silent.

## **The Empty Room**

In **63 BC** a Roman general named **Pompey** walked into the Holy of Holies. The room only the high priest entered, one day a year. He pulled the curtain back to see what the Jews were hiding in there.

### **And do you know what he found? Absolutely nothing.**

The room had been empty for **five hundred years**. The ark vanished when Babylon burned the first temple. 

They came home. They rebuilt. Same room. Same curtain. Nothing behind it. 

The rabbis had a theology about it. *When Haggai and Zechariah and Malachi died, they said the Holy Spirit departed from Israel*. 

What was left was a f**loor and a curtain**. Once a year the high priest went in and stood there to make atonement. 

No cloud by day. No fire by night. No glory filling the house so the priests could not even walk in. Just a room.

Pompey walked inside and took a stroll. He described it. A Roman historian wrote it down for the whole empire. The shrine was empty. No image. Nothing in the secret place.

But if you are standing there as a Jew, that is not the scandal. 

#### **The scandal is that he walked out.**

Uzzah touched the ark and dropped. Nadab and Abihu offered strange fire and they were done for. Pompey walks into the holiest room on earth — and the sky says nothing.

#### **So that’s who heard this gospel first. People with stories, an empty room, and the last thing they’d heard from heaven was a threat.**

## **The Turn**

*How does Mark answer this empty feeling*?

## **He doesn’t do it with a whisper.**

You have a group of believers. You’re meeting in a rented room over a shop. Maybe a barn. Or a warehouse. Or somebody's courtyard.

We have Bibles everywhere. On our shelves. In the pew. On the nightstand. A whole library of translations on the phone in our pocket. If I say “Isaiah 40,” you can have it open before I finish the sentence.

*They could not.*

There may have been a scroll in that room. But there was no Bible in every lap. No personal copy tucked under every arm. No chapters. No verse numbers. No page for the congregation to follow.

For most of them, the gospel did not arrive as words they could look down and read.

### **It arrived as a voice.**

A man stands up in the middle of the room. He’s been trained to do this. The ancient writers tell us what these were like. 

* Over the top.   
* Full of: Emotion. Gestures. Volume. 

A pace that does not let up. His voice has to get over the noise of the street, over the crying baby, over the man hammering in the next room. He is not reading to them. He is embodying a story.

#### **And here's how he opens.**

***\*The beginning.\**** **(Mark 1:1)**

Two words in and he has already put you in **Genesis**.

And then the next word out of his mouth is ***\*euangelion.\****

That is not a church word yet. That is an empire word. ***\*Euangelion\**** is what the herald shouts when the army wins. It's what runs ahead of the news that a son has been born to Caesar.

Someone dug up a stone from a town called Priene, dated nine years before Christ, and carved on it is a decree that says **the birthday of the god Augustus was the beginning of the good news for the world**.

He takes their headline and he just puts a different name in it.

***The beginning of the good news of Jesus Christ, the Son of God.***

*Do you see what he just did*? 

* Augustus is not the good news.   
* Rome is not the savior.   
* Caesar is not the son of God.   
* There is another King, and you’re about to hear His story.  
* He is not one more source of good news.  
* **He is the good news.**  
* He is not one more king asking for your allegiance.  
* **He is the King.**  
* He is not one more savior promising to hold your life together.  
* **He is Jesus Christ, the Son of God.**  
* Every other power will eventually take more than it gives. Caesar will ask you to die so he can keep his throne.  
* But before Mark’s story is finished, this King will give His own life for His subjects.

Look at the first word. **\*The beginning.\*** 

This is not just page one of a book. *Mark is saying*: 

#### **“Everything you have been waiting for, everything the prophets were pointing at, starts here. “**

Not the beginning of the gospel of Mark. *The beginning of the good news itself*. The greatest story ever told, and this is where it opens.

### **And where does he go first?** 

He says, ***\*as it is written in Isaiah the prophet.\****

And then, watch what he does. Because the first words out of his mouth are not **Isaiah**.

### **Mark names Isaiah, then starts weaving passages together.**

1. The Exodus road.  
2. Malachi’s messenger.  
3. Isaiah’s comfort.

**“Behold, I send my messenger before your face.”** That is **Exodus 23**. God sending His messenger ahead of Israel on the first Exodus, guarding them on the way into promise.

**“Who will prepare your way.”** That is **Malachi 3**. The messenger before the Lord. And the messenger is named **Malachi 4**: **“I will send you Elijah.**” Elijah was transported to Heaven at the time of Malachi, but they were expecting a prophet like him. To do what, exactly? 

Mark tells us: ***“A voice of one crying in the wilderness, Prepare the way of the Lord.” Isaiah 40***.

And if the hearers knew **Isaiah 40**, then they probably remembered how it started:

***“Comfort. Comfort my people.”***

The last word before the silence was a curse. *Herem.* Devoted to destruction.

### **Then four hundred years pass.**

And when the silence finally breaks, Mark reaches for a chapter that begins with comfort.

But notice how that comfort arrives.

Not first as a throne.

Not first as an army.

Not even first as the King Himself.

### **It arrives as a voice out ahead of Him.**

Because kings did not arrive unannounced. Before the king appeared, someone went ahead of him with one message:

*Clear the road. Get ready. The king is coming.*

That man was called a herald.

### **We don’t really have heralds anymore. So let me show you what one looked like.**

**April 18, 1775\. Boston.**

He's a silversmith. He didn’t write laws. He never fired a shot in the war. He makes teapots and buckles and false teeth for a living.

But he's part of a network. Thirty men who spy on the British garrison and pass what they see. And that night the word comes up through the network that seven hundred regulars were moving out. 

#### **So they send him.**

He arranges a backup first.   
Two lanterns in the steeple of the Old North Church, so Charlestown gets the warning even if he doesn't make it out of Boston.   
Then two friends row him across the harbor, right past a British warship sitting there in the dark with sixty-four guns on it, close enough to hear the men on deck. They get him to the other side. There's a horse waiting.

And then he does the only thing he knows how to do.

#### **He rides.**

No strategy. No plan. No pamphlet, no petition, no committee. A horse, a road, and a throat that is going to be raw by morning.

He is not galloping down the middle of the road hollering. He gets to a house, he beats on the door, and when a man comes to the window half asleep, he tells him low and fast: the regulars are out tonight.

Then he's gone. Next house. Same thing. The regulars are out tonight.

*Thirteen miles to Lexington*. Doors and windows and one message the whole way.

#### **Get up. It's happening tonight.**

And that's what a herald is, church. 

* He didn't build anything.   
* He didn't shoot anyone.   
* He wasn't the commanding the army.   
* All he had was a road, a Word, and the willingness to wake people up who did not want to be woken up.

## **The Man at the Jordan**

Now come back to the desert with me, because God's herald is stranger than Boston's.

Paul Revere rode to the people. He went house to house, door to door, thirteen miles of knocking.

### **John doesn’t knock. He preaches.** 

***Mark chapter 1, verse 4***.   
***"And so John the Baptist appeared in the wilderness, preaching a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins."***

*He appeared in the wilderness*. 

Now let’s go back to the text. Mark just quoted **Isaiah 40**, a voice crying ***\*in the wilderness\****. 

**And the very next sentence, there's a man standing** \*in the wilderness\*, crying out. That's the first Easter egg paying off. 

The Scripture didn't just get quoted. It got up and walked out of the scroll and put on camels hair and yelled repent. The comfort chapter has a face now.

He stands out in the wild, out where the leopards and the wolves and the hyenas are, and **verse 5** says t**he whole Judean countryside and \**all the people of Jerusalem*\* went out to him.**

Why? Because the traffic follows the voice. And after four hundred years, the voice wasn't in the temple. It was in the wilderness. 

And look at which water he's standing in. 

### **The Jordan.** 

That's not a random creek. 

* *That is the water Israel walked through to enter the promised land the first time*.   
* That is the water Elijah split with his rolled-up mantle on his last day on earth.   
* That river is the front door of the whole story.   
* And John is standing in the doorway saying: He’s coming\! Repent\! He’s coming\!

Isaiah said the messenger would:

* **prepare the way**.   
* Level the mountains.   
* Straighten the crooked places.   
* Fill in the valleys. 

His message: Repent. \****Metanoia***\*. *Turn around*. 

### **Do you recognize that job description?** 

A baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins. 

#### **That is Malachi's messenger doing exactly what Malachi said he would do.** 

Turning hearts.   
Calling the nation back before the judgment falls.   
That's the second Easter egg paying off. 

The refining has started, and notice it doesn't start with Rome. 

#### **It starts with us, one confessed sin at a time.**

And that’s exactly what they’re doing, **verse 5: \*confessing their sins\***, out loud, in the water, in front of everybody. That's a mountain getting pulled down. That's a crooked place going straight. Every man and woman coming up out of that water dripping wet just cleared a lane for the King.

## **The Wardrobe**

And look at what Mark does here, because he does something he hardly ever does: he stops. Mark is the gospel in a hurry. You read it and you'll find *immediately* this, *immediately* that, over and over — he doesn't slow down often. But right here he spends time to describe what John is wearing.

In **verse 6** it says he wore *clothing made from camel's hair,* with a l*ittle leather belt around his waist*. The coat tells you it's a prophet. That rough camel-hair coat was basically the prophet's uniform. 

It went all the way back to Elijah, and the prophets after him wore the same kind of thing — so when you saw a man come out of the wild in a coarse hairy garment, your mind went straight to one word: *prophet*.

### **But John isn’t the point.** 

Mark makes sure we know that when we hear him speak. ***Verse 7: "After me comes one more powerful than I, the straps of whose sandals I'm not worthy to stoop down and untie."*** 

A disciple would do almost anything for his rabbi — carry his stuff, cook his meals, wait on him hand and foot. But when it comes to handling the master's filthy, road-caked sandals, that job belongs to a slave. And John, with all those people standing in the river and lining the bank, says: the one coming behind me — I don't even rate high enough to stoop down and touch His shoelaces.

Frauds point at themselves. Heralds point to Jesus.

Then he says in ***verse 8***, 

***"I baptize you with water, but He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit."*** 

I know we've heard that all our lives. But in that period, in second-temple Judaism, the temple was missing these things:

1. **The ark**  
2. **The fire**  
3. **The glory**  
4. **The Holy Spirit**

#### **The prophets said the Spirit had departed from Israel.** 

Remember, Pompey walked into that room, the holiest place on earth — and nothing happened. It was an empty, bare floor. Now hear what Mark is saying: He is coming, and He's bringing back the Spirit. But He's not just gonna put Him in a room. He's gonna put the Spirit in *you*.

Two verses later, Jesus comes up out of that water, and Mark doesn't say the sky opens. Matthew says it opens. Luke says it opens. 

***10 “Just as Jesus was coming up out of the water, he saw heaven being torn open and the Spirit descending on him like a dove.” (Mark 1:10)***

**Mark says the sky was *torn* — ripped like a fabric — and the Spirit comes down and lands on Jesus.**

But is Mark really trying to tell us something? Well… the only other time we see this word for **"torn"** is fifteen chapters later, when Jesus dies. The veil of the temple is ripped from top to bottom. Torn. 

***38 The curtain of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom. 39 And when the centurion, who stood there in front of Jesus, saw how he died,\[[c](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%2015&version=NIV#fen-NIV-24866c)\] he said, “Surely this man was the Son of God\!” (Mark 15:38-39)***

That same curtain Pompey pulled back, that hung over an empty room — God Himself tears it. The sky was torn at the water. The veil was torn at the cross. 

And it was the same rip.

God's answer wasn't another filled room. It was a filled people. 

Paul writes to a church and says, ***"Don't you know that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost?"*** ***(1 Corinthians 6:19-20)***

At Pentecost, fire fell on **120** people in an upper room. And it hasn't stopped falling since.

After 400 years of silence, God didn't just say something.

*He moved in.*

## **Get Dressed**

### **The Belt**

### **There's a story in 2nd Kings chapter 1\.** 

King Ahaziah is hurt, and he sends men to ask a Philistine god if he's gonna live or not. Elijah stops them on the road and tells them to turn around. They go back to the king and tell him about it. The king says, **"What did the guy look like?**" They say, **"He's a hairy man with a leather belt around his waist."** And the king says, **"That's Elijah the Tishbite."**

*He recognized him by the belt.*

#### **Malachi closes the Old Testament, not just with a curse but with a promise: *"I will send you Elijah."*** 

**Mark starts this Gospel by quoting the Old Testament that says, *"I know you feel like you're cursed, but comfort, comfort my people — I'm sending you Elijah."* *And then Mark describes John the Baptist giving the exact description of Elijah*.**

In that world, everybody wore long, loose robes. But you cannot run in a robe. You cannot work in a robe. You cannot fight in a robe. So when it was time to move, a man would grab all that loose, trailing fabric, pull it up, and cinch it tight in his belt so his legs could swing free. They had a phrase for it. 

***\*Girding up your loins.\**** 

#### **Girding Your Loins**

It meant one thing: *something is about to go down, get ready to move*.

Now look at the wardrobe of Israel that day.

* Herod's court is wearing soft silk that trails along palace floors.   
* The temple priests are wearing elaborate sashes draped over long linen robes.   
* Beautiful clothes. Clothes for standing still. *Clothes for a world where nothing ever changes.*

#### **And one man in the whole country is cinched up.** 

* Coarse camel hair pulled tight in a workman's leather belt.   
* Dressed like a man who's expecting something.   
* Dressed like a man in Israel who actually believed God was about to move.

**Isaiah 11:5** says of the Messiah Himself, 

***"Righteousness shall be the belt of His waist, and faithfulness the belt of His loins."***

And then Paul takes that very verse and hands it to the church in **Ephesians 6*:14***

***“Stand therefore, \*having girded your loins with truth”***

In the Greek it's the same word

#### **So gird up.** 

Pull up everything loose and trailing in your life, every compromise dragging the ground behind you, every habit you keep tripping over, and cinch it in the belt of truth. 

Because heralds don't get to lounge in silk. **There are doors to knock on**.

## **The Altar**

Now let me ask you what I asked you at the beginning, but let me ask it about you this time.

### **Do you know what it's like to live inside a silence?**

You've prayed and stared at the ceiling. You've done everything you know to do and heaven feels shut over your house. 

* Maybe it's been quiet over your marriage.   
* Quiet over that son or that daughter.   
* Quiet over the diagnosis.   
* Quiet over the habit you swore you'd have beat by now.   
* Quiet since you buried somebody.   
* And the last word you remember hearing sounded an awful lot like a threat, and you've been doing what Israel did for four hundred years.   
* Guessing.   
* Is God gone? Is God weak? Or is God angry at me?

Hear me this morning. They asked that for four centuries, and the whole time, the whole time, God was neither gone nor weak nor done. And when He finally spoke, He didn't whisper and He didn't send a memo. He tore the sky open and moved in.

**The silence over your life is not the absence of God.**

The wilderness you are standing in right now might just be the road He's coming down. It usually is. He loves to show up in the desert.

*And here's the difference between you and them*. They waited four hundred years for the Voice. You don't have to wait, because the Voice already came, and the sky already tore, and the veil is already ripped, and the Spirit is still falling on bodies. He does not leave rooms empty anymore.

#### **This altar is open.**

Come. Come like Jerusalem came, out of your routine, out of the beautiful empty box you've been keeping up, down to the water.

Come confess it out loud like they did. That's the roadwork. That's the mountain coming down.

If heaven's been quiet over your home, come.

If you've been guessing at God for years, come.

If there's a room in you that's been bare floor and hung-up curtain for as long as you can remember, come, because He fills empty rooms, that's the whole good news.

And if you're saved and you're full and you know it, then come anyway, and gird up, because you're the rider now, and the night is full of houses.

Come. The King is out. He is moving.
