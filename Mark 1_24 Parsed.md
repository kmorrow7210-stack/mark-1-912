### Syntax

**Τί ἡμῖν καὶ σοί;** is a Semitic-style idiom:  
“What is there between us and you?”  
In natural English:  
“What do you want with us?”“What have we to do with you?”  
**ἦλθες ἀπολέσαι ἡμᾶς;**  
The infinitive **ἀπολέσαι** expresses the purpose of Jesus’ coming:  
“Have you come to destroy us?”  
**οἶδά σε τίς εἶ**  
This is literally:  
“I know you—who you are.”  
The clause **τίς εἶ** identifies the content of what the spirit knows:  
“I know who you are.”  
**ὁ ἅγιος τοῦ θεοῦ** stands in apposition, naming Jesus’ identity:  
“the Holy One of God.”  
Notice the shift from plural **ἡμῖν / ἡμᾶς** (“us”) to singular **οἶδα** (“I know”). The individual spirit speaks while identifying itself with the wider demonic opposition.

**Φιμώθητι** literally means:  
“Be muzzled\!”  
It is stronger than a polite “be quiet.” Jesus commands the spirit to be completely silenced.  
The two aorist imperatives demand decisive action:  
“Be silenced, and come out of him\!”  
A smooth translation of both verses:  
“Saying, ‘What do you want with us, Jesus the Nazarene? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are—the Holy One of God\!’ But Jesus rebuked him, saying, ‘Be silenced, and come out of him\!’”
